Set up measurements and permissions

Allow only the measurements you want. Setup prepares access; it does not start a scan.

Important to know

Permission, hardware readiness, network reachability, and device visibility are different things. A readiness checkmark is not a safety verdict.

What to try

  1. In Spectra, open Settings. Under Privacy & storage, choose Measurement access to review permissions and sensor readiness. Tap Done to return; unused measurements do not need to be configured.
  2. For Access not allowed, change Spectra’s access in iPhone Settings, then return to Spectra for a fresh access check. Retrying cannot add unsupported hardware or override an iOS restriction; follow its guidance or choose different measurements.
  3. Return to Spectra, choose measurements, and review any readiness message before starting.

Supported iPhones

Spectra requires an iPhone running iOS 27 or later. Scanning, Bluetooth Proximity, saved results, and JSON export do not require Apple Intelligence or an in-app purchase. Each measurement still needs its hardware and permission; Spectra checks selected measurements before a scan and explains anything unavailable.

Start with access

Welcome explains the app. Get Started opens measurement setup. Tap a measurement to request its access; a readiness checkmark reflects the system response. You can continue without every permission. A prompt explains what remains unconfigured, and a later check requests only missing access.

Choose measurements for each check

Quick Check uses Bluetooth and optional Local network for 30 seconds. Area Sweep can use Bluetooth, Local network, and Motion and magnetic, together or independently, with an extendable 30-minute limit. Monitor is stationary and radio-only. Smart Glasses and Watches & Wearables keep Bluetooth fixed; their short preparation screen offers the same optional Tag location choice as other iPhone checks. Specialist results offer name search and a separate View observed activity route for Bluetooth identities that did not match. Device types are estimates, not selectable filters.

No local network available?

Local Network needs a joined Wi-Fi or supported wired local link and permission. Spectra offers Check again when readiness can be rechecked, or continuation without that measurement when others can run. Returning from Settings rechecks availability; it does not silently start a scan. An internet connection is not required for local discovery.

Read Coverage

A completed source ran for the available check window, not that every device was found. Partial means coverage was interrupted or quality-limited. Unavailable means the source could not run; Not selected means you excluded it. Read the accompanying reason before drawing conclusions. Observation handling counts are not physical-device counts or available disk space.

Remember where a check began

Before any iPhone check, optionally turn on Tag location. It is off for each new check and asks for Location access only when chosen. Approximate access is supported; scanning still works if permission is denied or a usable fix is unavailable. Spectra requests a single position near collection start and stops requesting it after a short window. Scan start location shows coordinates and estimated accuracy, not an address, route, room, or detected-device position. You can separately enable automatic map previews of saved areas through Apple Maps; this does not request your current location. Watch captures do not receive the phone’s location when imported. Location is not required for Bluetooth, local-network, or magnetic measurements.

Apple Intelligence is optional

Only on-device analysis requires an Apple Intelligence-capable iPhone with Apple Intelligence enabled and ready in a supported language and region. If your iPhone does not support it, or the model is disabled or still downloading, you can still scan, review all measured evidence, and export JSON. No analysis runs automatically. Availability is checked separately when you use analysis.

Take your time, with a clear stopping point

The iPhone Area Sweep limit starts when collection begins, not while permissions or instructions are open. At 25 minutes, a quiet notice offers Extend 30 Minutes or Finish Area Sweep. Each extension adds 30 minutes to the deadline, with another notice five minutes before it. Elapsed time stays visible. At the deadline, Spectra finishes through its normal results flow and follows Save completed sessions; it does not override your saving preference. Confirm the saved status or use Retry Saving or Export JSON if needed. Monitor and the Watch’s 30-second sweep are unchanged. Longer listening does not guarantee complete discovery or unlimited retained evidence.

Start from the Home Screen

Touch and hold Spectra’s app icon, then choose Quick Check, Smart Glasses, or Watches & Wearables. Quick Check opens measurement selection; the specialist shortcuts open their Bluetooth-only preparation screen. Location tagging stays optional and off until you choose it. On first use, finish Welcome and measurement setup, then explicitly start your requested check. Canceling clears that request. If a check is still open, including its completed results, or another task is presented over the app, Spectra preserves it instead of starting another scan; close it and choose the shortcut again. Shortcuts do not enable background scanning or completion sounds.

Choose an app icon

In Spectra, open Settings → App icon to choose Classic, Spectra Blue, Light, or Blue on White. Each uses Spectra’s original waveform. A checkmark identifies the icon confirmed by iOS; a spinner shows a change in progress. This changes the app icon, not the colors inside Spectra. Your iPhone’s icon appearance settings may adjust the colors. Reset Spectra erases local data and returns to Welcome first, then asks iOS to restore Classic. If iOS takes longer than expected, Spectra reports the unconfirmed change; iOS may still finish an earlier request. Review the icon in Settings after setup.

Technical detail

Why does Wi-Fi name ask for Location?

Showing an optional Wi-Fi name uses Apple’s Access Wi-Fi Information rules, including Precise Location While Using the App when required. This is not needed for Bluetooth, magnetic exploration, or Bonjour browsing. The Wi-Fi-name action does not request geographic coordinates or save the name. Tag location is a separate, per-check request for coordinates; granting permission alone does not enable it. iOS may withhold a Wi-Fi name even when local discovery can operate.

When can a location tag be saved?

Spectra accepts a fix at most ten seconds old and waits up to twelve seconds from collection start. A fix must report estimated horizontal accuracy of 5 km or better. Approximate permission can produce a broad region, and indoor reception may yield no usable fix. These are freshness and quality bounds, not a guarantee of accuracy. No automatic address lookup is performed. If no fix qualifies, the check continues without a tag. Starting another check never reuses a prior session’s position as its own.

A Wi-Fi name is not a router identity

The displayed network name provides connection context, not a router model, physical location, or assurance that the network is trustworthy. Guest networks and client isolation may prevent discovering other clients despite a working connection.

Why is a saved signal not available?

Bluetooth Proximity listens for new broadcasts. A private reference retained with a new iPhone capture can recognize the original framework identity after reopening; it cannot make a silent or rotating advertiser appear. Older or imported sessions and Watch captures may need a confirmed possible match. If no useful name or supported payload clue was retained and no private reference is available, Spectra explains that limitation rather than starting an indistinguishable search. Bluetooth access and finding the signal are separate requirements. A saved signal strength is not a current reading.

Text size and accessibility

Spectra uses a fixed standard text size. Changing iOS Dynamic Type or Larger Text does not resize the app’s own interface. VoiceOver labels remain available, and contrast and reduced-motion preferences still apply. System-owned permission dialogs and sharing interfaces are controlled by iOS.

What works without internet?

The shipped device catalog, Guide, Bluetooth observation, magnetic exploration, saved history, and preparing JSON work on device. Apple Intelligence analysis also works locally once its supported model is ready. Local Network still needs a usable local link. Loading a map preview, sending email, opening website help, cloud file destinations, or using an external AI service may require internet. An unavailable map does not prevent reviewing saved coordinates or findings.

How detection works

Spectra helps you investigate nearby signals. A signal is a clue—not proof of a hidden device.

Important to know

An observation does not prove that a device is recording or malicious. A quiet check cannot prove that a space is clear.

What to try

  1. Choose a 30-second radio Quick Check, a self-paced Area Sweep with an extendable 30-minute limit, or Monitor to watch one area.
  2. Choose measurements; Quick Check records every supported category. Filter completed results by recorded measurement or search names, or use a Bluetooth-only specialist check. Keep Spectra visible while scanning.
  3. Review what stood out, then open Coverage to see what was—and was not—measured.

Observe without connecting

Spectra reads broadcasts, advertised network services, and iPhone sensor measurements. It does not pair with or open sessions to discovered devices.

Built to keep getting better

We regularly improve Spectra’s device catalog and detection engine to help you make better sense of nearby signals. These improvements are included in app updates at no additional charge. Install updates through the App Store; there is no separate catalog download or subscription. Newer recognition can also help you revisit eligible saved sessions with Recheck session, without changing the original. An update does not guarantee a match or make every device detectable.

Start with the summary

Summary surfaces named findings and supported possible device types, even when the manufacturer or model is unknown. Each result keeps its confidence or name-source label. Related observations stay together; other unnamed activity stays compact. Expand a result for its measurements and supporting evidence.

Recognize a network role without an exact device

Local-network discovery can reveal supported printing, scanning, file sharing, media, automation, and imaging or audio services. A service describes what an endpoint advertises, not necessarily a physical device: a computer can host a print queue, and an imaging service may also belong to audio hardware. Reviewed names and protocol-specific model fields can support a Possible product or family. Without those clues, Spectra keeps the service role and its uncertainty visible instead of inventing a model.

A useful clue without an exact model

A Possible smartwatch or Possible audio output can appear without a product name when reviewed broadcast evidence supports that category. A result shows up to three retained possible interpretations; Technical details holds the remaining retained interpretations and their sources. These possibilities are not an exhaustive list of products. The Conflicting identity claims label means Spectra has not selected a product. Multiple interpretations do not create extra devices or increase review priority. The same eligible finding stays available for Bluetooth Proximity and analysis; neither verifies its identity.

Check for glasses or wearables

Choose Smart Glasses or Watches & Wearables from Specialist checks for a 30-second Bluetooth observation. Watches & Wearables includes supported smartwatches, fitness bands and trackers, smart rings, and other wearables; glasses stay in their own check. Results include catalog matches, reviewed possible category evidence, and explicitly Possible indications from names such as Smart Watch, Smart Ring, Fitness Tracker, or Smart Frames. Names do not establish a manufacturer or model. Names without a supported type, accessories, generic services, and company codes alone do not qualify. Spectra cannot tell whether a microphone, camera, or conversation-summary feature is active.

Understand the wearable overview

During a wearable check, the overview counts findings by type. Repeated broadcasts from the same retained Bluetooth identity count once; rotating identities can still represent one physical device. After completion, Search names finds a particular supported match without changing the type counts. Device types are estimates, not selectable filters. View observed activity opens every other retained Bluetooth identity, including names whose type could not be determined. This separate context is not a specialist match and is excluded from specialist export, analysis, and proximity choices. Export, on-device analysis choices, and Bluetooth Proximity still include every eligible finding in the check, regardless of the display filters. Older sessions without a recorded wearable subtype appear under Other wearables without changing their saved product interpretation.

Watch for radio changes

Monitor compares Bluetooth and local-network observations with a starting baseline while your iPhone stays in one place. The dashboard retains new, returned, and no-longer-recent observations. Choose Stop Monitor to review details. Rotating or intermittent broadcasts can cause changes without a device arriving or leaving. Magnetic exploration belongs in Area Sweep.

Technical detail

Names are claims, not authentication

Product-family labels come from reviewed metadata and an offline catalog. Possible, Probable, and signature labels describe the evidence—not numeric probabilities. A System name comes from the collecting device’s Bluetooth framework and may be cached rather than transmitted in that observation. Names and payloads can be changed or spoofed.

Registry context is not a verified maker

Bluetooth company codes and member service numbers identify registered namespaces, sometimes belonging to a chip or protocol provider. Google-registered Bluetooth service, for example, means Google registered that service code—not that Google made the nearby device. Device unknown means Spectra has not identified the device. This is not an account-registration message or evidence that the device is communicating with Google. Technical details retain the service codes, parser outcomes, sources and registry versions. A Registry model uses a public Matter vendor/product record matched to unverified broadcast identifiers; it does not prove the hardware is genuine. Saved catalog and name-based interpretations remain recorded history. An eligible session can be explicitly rechecked with newer recognition, creating a separate copy without changing the original.

Useful clues inside a broadcast

Reviewed complete broadcasts can reveal a protocol or declared category without an exact product. Nearby Presence broadcast describes a public proximity-related format, not a particular phone or wearable. Possible phone · Nearby sharing broadcast means the reviewed sharing format declares a phone category; it does not identify a maker, model, owner or operating system. Possible HomeKit camera is an accessory-category claim, not proof that Apple made the device or that it is recording. Supported audio formats can suggest an audio accessory. These clues are unauthenticated and may be spoofed. Unknown or incomplete formats stay uninterpreted. Encrypted contents and account information stay opaque; rotating identifiers are not resolved into an owner or stable device identity.

Model clues without a broadcast name

Some complete broadcasts contain public product codes. Spectra can look up a Matter vendor/product tuple in its bundled public registry, or a supported MiBeacon product code in a reviewed model table. A public outer-header code may still be usable when the contents are encrypted; Spectra does not decrypt them. A Possible model or device type is a clue, not authenticated hardware. Supported sensor and automation formats may identify a family without a specific model. A code’s registered owner or ecosystem is not automatically the device’s manufacturer. Conflicting claims remain alternatives; saved findings retain the interpretation from that session until you explicitly recheck an eligible copy.

A protocol without a device type

Weave setup advertisement, Bluetooth Mesh setup advertisement, Bluetooth Mesh proxy advertisement, and AltBeacon proximity broadcast describe supported public formats, not a product, physical device type, owner, or activity. Weave vendor/product codes use their own namespace, not the Matter registry. Private identifiers and security-state details are not exposed as recognition clues. A complete Technical archive can still contain their raw bytes; review it before sharing.

Shared knowledge, separate observations

Reviewed product and family names help identify both Bluetooth and local-network observations. A recognizable Apple TV name, for example, can suggest the same family on either source. Protocol-specific model fields and byte signatures still require the matching protocol evidence. Bluetooth-only name decorations such as LE are not removed from ordinary network names. Sharing catalog knowledge does not merge the observations into one physical device or raise their confidence. A shared name, manufacturer, strength, or timing cannot establish that relationship. Magnetic observations are area clues, not device identities.

Reviewing an unfamiliar space

Use Spectra to inform your next decision—not to certify that a space is safe.

Important to know

For a credible threat or highly sensitive activity, do not rely on a phone scan. Move sensitive conversations to a more appropriate location and consult a trusted security contact. Do not tamper with equipment or confront people based on an app result.

What to try

  1. Choose Quick Check for a radio snapshot or Area Sweep for deliberate exploration. Do not join an unfamiliar Wi-Fi network just to scan.
  2. Review coverage, repeat observations from consistent positions, and consider ordinary explanations before escalating.
  3. Check unclassified and out-of-filter results. Expected is your label for this check, not authenticated safety.
  4. Confirm saving before closing; use Sessions to add your context. An earlier check does not describe the space now.

Hotels, rentals, and shared spaces

At a hotel, Airbnb or other vacation rental, or in student housing, start with a Quick Check to get oriented. Use Area Sweep for a closer, unhurried look at accessible areas you are allowed to inspect. In gyms and changing rooms, respect other people's privacy and facility rules; move to a private place to review findings. An unfamiliar broadcast is not evidence of covert recording, and a signal may come from outside the room.

Rideshares and busy public places

As a passenger, or while waiting in an airport, station, café, or other public space, a Quick Check can provide a snapshot of available signals. Specialist checks focus on recognizable glasses or wearables. Crowds contain many ordinary devices; names and signal strength cannot identify the person carrying one. Do not scan while driving or confront someone based on a result.

Check what was actually measured

Review partial or unavailable sources before interpreting results. A 30-second snapshot can miss intermittent broadcasts. Local Network reaches only visible services on your joined network.

Separate clues from conclusions

A product name can be changed or spoofed. Strong Bluetooth readings are not distance, and magnetic spikes can come from ordinary magnets or wiring. Neither identifies recording activity. Clear measurement filters and name search to include every retained result. In specialist checks, also open View observed activity; an unidentified device may not appear among supported matches.

Protect the connection, too

Confirm the intended Wi-Fi name with the venue using a trusted channel; a familiar name alone does not authenticate a network. Prefer cellular data when you do not trust the connection. Keep iOS and apps updated, protect accounts with multifactor authentication, and heed certificate warnings. HTTPS protects traffic to a site but does not prove that the site itself is trustworthy. Avoid exposing sensitive conversations or screens in shared spaces.

Where a VPN helps—and where it does not

If you use shared Wi-Fi, consider a reputable VPN you trust and can use under your organization's policy. It encrypts traffic routed through its tunnel between your phone and the VPN service; trust shifts to that provider. It does not prevent nearby cameras or microphones from recording, authenticate a hotspot, make you anonymous, or make a malicious website safe. Spectra does not provide a VPN. VPN settings can affect local-network discovery; do not turn off required protection just to find more devices.

Record the context

Rename a saved session and add notes about areas inspected, repeatable changes, alternative explanations, and unresolved questions. Notes are yours, not sensor evidence. Avoid sensitive itinerary or personal details.

Technical detail

Not a forensic record

Reports and notes are bounded local records, not authenticated exports or a forensic chain of custody. The primary history file keeps at most 500 sessions within 256 MiB. Saved sessions are not automatically removed; export and delete copies you no longer need to make room. A matching archive checksum does not authenticate its author, collector, or contents. Temporary replacement files and protected recovery copies may use additional disk space. Spectra marks local history for exclusion from device backups; iOS controls backup and restore behavior. Files you export may be backed up by your chosen destination.

Beyond the iPhone's visibility

Silent, wired, shielded, powered-off, or unrecognizable devices can remain invisible. Identity rotation can produce more than one exact record, and the app does not merge devices across sessions. No checklist or result is an all-clear.

Try the Experimental Watch collector

Capture without your iPhone nearby. Save up to five Bluetooth sweeps on your Watch, then review findings on your paired iPhone. This companion is experimental—not a background scanner.

Important to know

Experimental means behavior is still being validated across Watches and environments. Bluetooth identifiers are not a verified device count. An empty or partial capture is never an all-clear.

What to try

  1. Install the Watch companion and open Spectra on iPhone once. Set the Watch’s Wake Duration to 70 seconds.
  2. Tap to wake the Watch fully, start a sweep, and keep Spectra active for the 30-second window.
  3. Review completion or interruption status. New captures are saved and queued for your paired iPhone automatically.
  4. Open Spectra on iPhone and review Sessions once processing finishes. Review findings and Coverage before drawing conclusions; use Settings → Experimental Watch if a transfer needs attention.

One check, a smaller screen

The optional Experimental Watch companion requires watchOS 27 or later and a paired iPhone running iOS 27 or later with Spectra. The iPhone app works without an Apple Watch. The companion offers only a 30-second Bluetooth Area Sweep. It does not measure local-network services or magnetic fields, run specialist checks, tag locations, or identify devices on the Watch. New captures queue for transfer to your paired iPhone, where Spectra identifies retained signals and saves the result in Sessions when active. Importing does not attach the phone’s current location.

Leave your iPhone behind, review later

After installing the companion and opening Spectra on your paired iPhone once to prepare transfer, you can collect without your iPhone nearby. The Watch uses its own Bluetooth radio and saves captures locally; collection does not need an internet connection or cellular plan. Keep Bluetooth available, allow access, and keep Spectra active with the display fully awake for each 30-second sweep. Check Saved sweeps before heading out: the Watch holds up to five captures in total, including earlier copies. When you reconnect, open Spectra on your paired iPhone to identify signals and review findings in Sessions. The Watch shows capture and transfer status, not identified results.

Keep the display fully awake

On Apple Watch, open Settings → Display & Brightness → Wake Duration and choose Wake for 70 Seconds. Tap to wake the display fully, start the sweep, and keep your wrist raised with Spectra active until it finishes. The first tap on Start Sweep after launching Spectra shows a setup reminder: Start Sweep continues; Not Now starts nothing. Acknowledging it does not verify or change your setting. Always On can leave Spectra visible while inactive; visibility alone does not keep Bluetooth collection running. Return to Clock is a different setting. Spectra cannot change these system settings or guarantee active time on every Watch.

An interruption keeps a partial capture

If Spectra becomes inactive or you leave it during collection, the sweep ends and preserves the evidence already retained as partial. It does not resume when you return. An early stop, Bluetooth loss, or a retention limit can also reduce coverage. Review the reason, adjust your setup, and start a new sweep when ready. A timer finishing does not prove every nearby device was observed.

Finish on Watch, review on iPhone

Open Spectra on your paired iPhone once to prepare transfer. After each new sweep, the Watch saves the capture before automatically queuing it for your paired iPhone. Queued means delivery is pending; Received on iPhone confirms local storage, not completed recognition. Open Spectra on iPhone to identify received signals and add them to Sessions automatically. No need to keep the Watch completion screen open. Choose New Sweep to collect again, or open Saved sweeps from the tray button to review transfer status and retry when needed. Transfers may wait for connectivity or Apple’s scheduling. Settings → Experimental Watch on iPhone provides processing and transfer help. Older captures sent before automatic handoff may still offer Add to Sessions.

Find your Watch sweep in Sessions

Look for the Watch-with-radio-waves icon and Apple Watch · Bluetooth-only sweep label. They identify where the evidence was collected, not a search for nearby watches. Your custom name never replaces this origin label, and rechecks keep it along with the original capture time. Experimental and partial-capture status stay visible. Blue icons identify original captures; violet icons and a Recheck number identify later interpretations. Review Coverage: even a complete 30-second capture cannot confirm that every nearby device was observed.

Room for the next sweep

Each device retains up to five capture copies, bounded to 4 MiB each. When space is needed, the Watch retires only copies confirmed received on iPhone; the phone retires only inbox copies already processed into Sessions. Unsent or unprocessed evidence is not automatically discarded. Saved Sessions are not automatically removed to make room. If session history is full, remove sessions you no longer need and retry phone processing; the unprocessed inbox copy remains. A full capture store with no eligible copies stops accepting new captures; review transfer or processing status before retrying. If capture storage cannot load, use Retry Loading before choosing erasure. It rechecks local storage without erasing copies; transfer or processing failures have their own retry actions. Watch sweeps save to Sessions independently of the Save completed sessions setting for iPhone checks.

Captured on Watch, reviewed on iPhone

Captures can contain advertised names, manufacturer and service bytes, signal strength, timestamps, and session-scoped identity tokens. These fields can identify devices or people. Spectra uses protected local storage marked for exclusion from backups and Apple’s paired-device transfer; it does not upload captures to Arc Signal or an identification service. Operating-system backup behavior remains under Apple’s control.

A focused start, help when you need it

The Watch start screen keeps Start Sweep as the main action. The tray button opens Saved sweeps for captures and transfer status. Open Settings below Start Sweep for Sweep guidance, Storage & transfer, Diagnostic reports, and About. About shows the version, release identifier, numeric build and watchOS version installed on your Watch; check these separately from iPhone when troubleshooting an update. Recognition uses the engine and catalog installed on iPhone, not a separate Watch catalog.

Choose completion feedback

Completion haptic in Spectra’s Watch Settings is on by default. Turn it off if you prefer visual feedback only. The system feedback follows your Watch sound and haptic settings; it is not a guarantee of silent operation. Read the completion or partial-capture status: feedback alone does not mean that every nearby device was observed or that transfer finished.

Confirm a reset on both devices

Reset Spectra on iPhone erases its sessions, received Watch data, and preferences, then requests erasure of captures, diagnostics, and preferences in the known paired Watch app. Watch reset pending means the Watch has not confirmed erasure; an offline Watch may still hold data. Keep the devices paired and open Spectra on Watch to finish. Watch reset confirmed appears only after the Watch reports completion. When the Watch receives a reset, it stops collection and discards any unfinished sweep. System permissions and files exported or shared elsewhere remain.

If the Watch reset is not ready

In Spectra on iPhone, open Settings and review the Apple Watch section. Watch reset not ready can mean the paired Watch app has not introduced itself or needs an update. Open or update Spectra on both devices, then explicitly choose Reset Watch and confirm. A newly paired or reinstalled Watch is not automatically erased by an earlier request. If Watch reset needs attention appears, follow the recovery guidance; do not assume erasure succeeded. No paired Watch means there is no Watch reset to request.

Technical detail

Observation is bounded

The Watch retains bounded advertisement evidence, not every radio packet. Callback timing is delivery timing, not proof of uninterrupted sampling. Repeated callbacks do not establish separate physical devices; a product can change identifiers. Names and protocol fields are unauthenticated claims. Recognition on iPhone cannot recover information the Watch did not retain. Recorded signal strength was measured by the Watch, not the iPhone; readings taken later with the phone can differ.

A new proximity check uses the iPhone

For an eligible saved Watch finding, Bluetooth Proximity searches for possible matching broadcasts near your iPhone and asks you to confirm a candidate. It cannot reuse a Watch identity as a verified iPhone target or turn recorded Watch strength into a live reading. Matching names or payloads can come from different devices. The new signal measurement is taken by the iPhone; it does not verify identity or measure distance from the Watch.

Copies have separate erase controls

Watch captures, the iPhone inbox, imported Sessions, and timing diagnostics are separate stores. Erase Inbox Copies leaves imported sessions intact. Individual session deletion leaves inbox and Watch copies intact. Erase All Sessions also clears the received Watch inbox, but does not request a Watch reset. Watch copies remain after those actions. Reset Spectra additionally clears received diagnostics and preferences and requests a paired Watch reset. Previously queued copies cannot silently restore erased iPhone data. Erasing captures directly on Watch cancels queued transfers where possible, but already delivered or in-flight phone copies may remain. Exported files and system permissions are not erased.

Why a small reset record remains

A protected local reset-status record survives a reset so Spectra can finish an offline Watch request and reject stale commands or transfers. It contains random coordination identifiers and request status, not captured signals. A reset request applies only to the Watch app installation it names. Repeated delivery of an already completed request does not erase a new sweep. Confirmation describes completion of that request, not a promise that the Watch can never collect new data.

Share timing diagnostics only when needed

Watch diagnostics are separate from scan captures. They retain up to 20 recent timing and count reports per device, including Watch model, OS/build, completion status and lifecycle events, but no nearby-device names, identity values or advertisement payloads. If diagnostic reports cannot load, choose Retry Loading on the affected device; it rechecks stored reports without erasing them. Use Share Watch diagnostics on iPhone to deliberately share received reports for troubleshooting. Dates and hardware details can still reveal context. Nothing is sent to support automatically.

What Bluetooth signal means

Use signal strength to compare observations—not to measure distance.

Important to know

A strong signal is not proof of a hidden or recording device. It does not necessarily belong to the closest device.

What to try

  1. Choose Check Bluetooth Proximity in results, then select a found device. Its individual details can open it directly.
  2. Start Signal Check to see live dBm readings. A saved result may require confirming a possible match; a name does not verify identity.
  3. Mark a stable reference, move a little, then pause in a similar phone orientation. Compare the live reading and the change in dB from your reference.

Read the band, not a distance

Strong, Moderate, and Faint describe the signal received by the collecting iPhone or Watch. A fresh Bluetooth Proximity check measures on your iPhone. Walls, bodies, reflections, antenna orientation, and transmit power can change the reading—even when neither device moves.

Network presence is different

A device found only through your joined Wi-Fi network has no measurable physical proximity in Spectra. Its network presence cannot tell you which room it is in.

Briefly or repeatedly observed

Briefly observed means only one or two usable Bluetooth signal readings were retained. Repeatedly observed means at least three were retained. These labels describe observation, not an authenticated device model or continuous presence. A weak or intermittent advertiser may produce only a brief sighting.

Follow a signal from a saved check

Bluetooth Proximity starts a fresh, unsaved listen; it never reuses an old reading as a live measurement. New Bluetooth captures saved on this iPhone can retain a private reference so their framework identities can be recognized after reopening, including from a recheck. A changed identity or silent device may still be unavailable. Older sessions, imported files, and Watch captures may offer possible matches based on a broadcast name, a separately labeled System name, or supported payload evidence. Confirm a suggestion before following it; similar names or bytes do not prove it is the same physical device.

Recognize nearby item trackers

Use Quick Check or Area Sweep with Bluetooth selected, or Monitor while stationary. Recognizable names for AirTag, Tile, Chipolo, Pebblebee, Galaxy SmartTag, and eufy SmartTrack can appear as Possible item-tracker matches. Finding-network accessory and Finding-network broadcast describe recognized formats, not a specific tracker; phones, computers and headphones may also participate. Open a finding for its evidence or select it for Bluetooth Proximity. These results describe nearby signal presence, not ownership or movement with you. Trackers may change identifiers or omit recognizable names, so Spectra cannot identify every tracker or rule one out when no match appears.

Technical detail

The underlying measurement

The live number shows received signal strength (RSSI) in dBm. Less-negative values are stronger: −40 dBm is stronger than −80 dBm. After ten seconds without a reading, the current value is replaced by No current reading; the previous value remains time-labeled briefly. Silence does not mean zero signal or prove a device has left. The reference comparison uses the median of up to six recent readings and shows the change in dB, not metres or a bearing. Technical details retain the earlier check’s signal evidence.

Several identities in one result

A similarity set shows the range across its currently observed Bluetooth identities rather than averaging unlike signals. Expand it to inspect each identity and its own measurement. A physical device may advertise more than one identity.

Why not precise Bluetooth ranging?

iOS 27 Channel Sounding requires a paired, connected accessory with compatible hardware. It cannot range arbitrary broadcasts. Spectra keeps discovery connection-free and compares received signal strength instead; it does not display invented meters or direction.

What finding-network labels mean

A complete Google Find Hub frame can support Finding-network accessory. The Apple offline-finding layout documented in published research can support Finding-network broadcast. These unauthenticated formats do not establish a manufacturer, model, lost-device state or reporting activity; programmable devices can imitate them. Spectra does not decode their identifiers, query an account, or connect to identify an AirTag. Raw fields and sources remain available in technical details.

Reading the magnetic meter

Actual Field shows the measurement. Δ Reference shows how far it has moved from your display reference.

Important to know

Speakers, magnets, metal, wiring, chargers, and phone cases can all change the field. A spike cannot identify a device or show its distance or intent. No change does not rule out a device.

What to try

  1. Choose Motion and magnetic for an Area Sweep. Remove magnetic cases, mounts, or attached accessories if practical, then establish a reference away from obvious magnetic sources.
  2. To get familiar with the response, use an ordinary speaker you own. Without touching it, slowly pass different parts of the phone over the same spot, holding the angle and gap steady. Repeat the path; do not press a strong magnet against the phone or open equipment. A flat response is inconclusive, not proof the sensor failed.
  3. Switch between Actual Field and Δ Reference to make changes easier to see.
  4. Review the event timeline for meaningful spikes, then revisit the area to see whether the change repeats.

Where to aim your iPhone

The magnetometer measures at one small sensor—not across the whole phone. Its location varies by iPhone model. Move slowly over the same spot, keeping the phone’s angle and gap consistent.

Example: iPhone 17 Pro Max

On iPhone 17 Pro Max, use the lower back near the USB-C end, not the camera area. Apple’s dimensional drawing marks the compass in this lower-back area. This is a model-specific location, not a universal iPhone scanning point. The camera and contactless-payment area are not a reliable guide to magnetometer placement.

Practice before an unfamiliar-space sweep

Try a practice sweep with an ordinary speaker you own before exploring an unfamiliar space. Keep a gap; do not press magnets against the phone. No change does not rule out a device. In the app, choose Practice with the magnetometer here or before beginning a magnetic Area Sweep, then tap Start Practice. Compare a familiar speaker with a spot away from electronics, repeat the same slow pass, and watch Actual Field and Δ Reference. Learn which phone positions produce a repeatable response without expecting a particular number. This is familiarization, not calibration or a pass/fail test of the phone. Practice uses live magnetic readings and motion context only: no Bluetooth, local-network discovery, device findings, exports, or saved session. Stop Practice, Done, or leaving the app clears the practice readings. Returning does not resume measurement; tap Start Practice to try again. Begin a separate Area Sweep with a fresh reference before reviewing a space.

Near a device, but no change?

Some devices produce little detectable magnetic change. Sensor position, distance, shielding, and magnetic accessories can also affect the response. No change does not rule out a device. A speaker’s magnet may cause a local change, but not every speaker or microphone produces a change the phone can resolve at your position. Watch Actual Field or Δ Reference, not just the saved event count. Confirm that live readings are arriving in Measurement Details; an unavailable or stale reading is not a quiet field.

Establish a comparison point

Pause away from obvious magnets or electronics so a stable display reference can form. Use Rebuild Reference to choose a fresh display comparison point. Automatic event detection has its own calibrated baseline.

Follow the calibration guidance

If prompted, make one gentle figure eight and then pause. If calibration stays limited, the actual-field trace can continue even though automatic spike marking is unavailable.

A fluctuation is not always an event

Automatic detection has its own baseline and adaptive threshold. A sustained change or an unusually strong spike can create an event. Ordinary small fluctuations should not.

Review the graph after finishing

Saved magnetic graph retains a downsampled, bounded view of display readings, independently of detector events. Choose Review graph in completed or saved results, switch Actual field or Δ Reference, and move the slider to inspect a reading. Reference changes and missing readings break the line. Older checks without graph data cannot recreate it.

Technical detail

Two different baselines

The display reference supports the actual/delta graph. The event detector independently evaluates calibrated samples against a noise-adaptive threshold with persistence and strong-spike rules. Rebuilding the display reference does not erase events or reset the detector.

What an event records

Events retain time, duration, measured field at the strongest change, signed change from the detector baseline, and threshold overage. These are magnetic excursions—not identified speakers, microphones, or other devices.

Measurement quality

Measurement Details exposes calibration, motion quality, and baseline status. Abrupt movement and unusable calibration remain excluded from event detection even while the actual-field trace is visible.

What the graph keeps

Half-second buckets preserve actual-field and delta extrema plus endpoints. At most 2,048 points are retained; long sweeps keep the most recent graph window and show an earlier-points-omitted notice. This is not a continuous recording. The graph does not determine which changes qualify as detector events.

How signals are grouped

Fewer cards make results easier to explore. They are not a count of confirmed physical devices.

Important to know

A shared name, vendor, or signal strength does not establish one physical device. Bluetooth and network identities are not merged across radios.

What to try

  1. Expand a Summary result to inspect its members and grouping explanation. Large groups offer Show all with their signal count: a searchable list of that group’s individual records, also available in specialist checks. Choose a signal to open its details. Matching results appear before related context; this list never merges similar signals into one device.
  2. Choose All Signals above Results for the exact retained inventory, or Show signals to expand unclassified activity. If Summary and All Signals look alike, the check may contain only separate identities without supported relationships.

Likely network endpoints

Several local-network services may appear together when their advertised relationship suggests the same endpoint. Expand the group to see its services and why they were linked.

Similar is not the same

Signals with similar broadcasts can share a similarity set. Its members may be separate devices; Spectra keeps their individual records. Observed pattern describes the strongest relevant member’s evidence tier, not identity confidence or proof that the signals belong together. The separate match badge and amber Similarity only caution still apply. Signal and service-signal counts describe retained records, not physical-device totals.

One list, with context on each result

Summary puts named findings and related signal profiles in one list, even when a match is only Possible or its device type is unknown. Each card shows its confidence and whether it represents one identity, likely related network services, or similar broadcasts that may be separate devices. Higher-evidence findings come first; items you marked expected stay at the end. Monitor distinguishes Since baseline, Starting baseline, and Earlier observations retained from before the current comparison. These sections describe observation time, not trusted or untrusted devices. The same names and device-type icons appear in analysis and proximity choices. A manufacturer code alone is not a device name. Unnamed, low-evidence activity stays expandable; All Signals retains every source identity and area clue. Visibility does not increase confidence or mark a finding as dangerous.

Hardware icons, separate confidence

Device icons describe a possible hardware category, not activity. A reviewed MacBook match can use a laptop; an uncertain subtype uses its supported parent category. Known electronic identities without a suitable specific icon use a chip. Conflicting product claims never borrow one model's icon. The confidence badge and source evidence remain separate: a more specific icon does not make a match stronger. A type already stated in reviewed text need not repeat; its Possible qualifier and source context still apply. A known product can still have no reviewed capability evidence. Names and icons do not verify a manufacturer, owner, recording or playback.

Unknown type, observed source

An otherwise unclassified radio finding uses a neutral source icon: radio waves for Bluetooth, a network for local-network observations, or a dotted circle for mixed or unavailable radio-source information. The icon does not identify hardware or imply Wi-Fi-wide scanning. Supported hardware or capability icons stay blue, including in similarity sets. The amber Similarity only caution still means the identities may be separate devices; blue does not make their relationship or identity certain.

Filter what was found

Use Filters to narrow results by the measurement that recorded them: Bluetooth, Local network, or Magnetic. Choose any combination and Apply; leave all unchecked to include everything. The button is hidden when only one measurement is represented. Search names looks for the displayed or broadcast name, including names whose device type is undetermined. It does not guess categories from your search. Cancel keeps the current measurement filter; Clear filters restores all results and clears name search. Device-type labels are helpful estimates, not exclusion rules. Filters and search never change collection, saved reports, or export scope.

Technical detail

Repeated observations

Repeated callbacks for the same exact framework identity accumulate in one record during a check. A rotating Bluetooth identifier can still create another record; the app does not assume it is the same physical device.

The local-network relationship

Bonjour grouping uses a session-scoped hint based on service instance, domain, and interface. It is evidence of a likely endpoint relationship, not authenticated hardware identity. Every service retains its exact record.

A new check starts fresh

Ordinary new checks use fresh session identifiers and do not automatically link devices across history. An explicit Bluetooth Proximity check can reuse a retained private reference for the selected iPhone capture; it does not merge sessions or authenticate hardware. Similarity sets and compact disclosures change presentation, not the underlying retained evidence.

Understand Monitor and its baseline

Keep the iPhone stationary and watch for observation changes—not a stream of device arrivals.

Important to know

Keep Spectra open. Locking the iPhone or switching apps ends measurement. Monitor is not an unattended or background security alarm.

What to try

  1. Choose Monitor and the radio measurements you need. Place the phone where it can stay.
  2. Wait for the starting comparison, then glance at the change summary. Do not interpret a quiet screen as an all-clear.
  3. Choose Stop Monitor to explore named/grouped results, repeat counts, change times, and any earlier comparisons.

Give the starting comparison time

Monitor starts establishing a baseline automatically. Keep the iPhone stationary: each available radio source needs 30 seconds of active observation. Permission waiting does not count. The countdown follows the slowest participating source. Observe 30 seconds longer extends that comparison once to 60 seconds per source. When the baseline is ready, the change counters start at zero and the panel shows its capture time and elapsed comparison time. No duration guarantees discovery of every device.

Initially observed does not mean trusted

Baseline signals remain in the final inventory. A potentially concerning device already present is not cleared or hidden. A device first observed during baseline collection becomes part of that starting comparison; one first observed afterward is newly observed, not necessarily newly arrived.

Read changes at a glance

The Newly observed, Returned, and Not recently seen rows count distinct signal identities in the current comparison’s latest 40 retained change events. Repeated changes for the same identity count once in each row. Counts may decrease as older events leave that bounded history. One identity can appear in more than one row, so do not add these counts as a total device count. Mark changes reviewed acknowledges the latest change without zeroing these counters, trusting a device, changing the baseline, or erasing evidence.

Choose a fresh comparison

Tap New Baseline in the main Monitor panel when you want a fresh reference, such as after moving the phone or changing networks. The tap starts a new 30-second observation window immediately; it does not simply clear the numbers. When the new baseline is ready, its change counters begin at zero. Earlier comparisons and their retained events remain in the same session for review after Stop Monitor. Wait for the current baseline to finish before starting another. Up to nine comparisons fit in one session; stop and start another Monitor when that limit is reached. Spectra does not automatically judge that you moved or silently replace your baseline.

Technical detail

Why did an identity disappear or return?

The Not recently seen label means that a previously observed identity has stopped being observed. For Bluetooth, Spectra waits 45 seconds without an observation before showing it. For local-network services, it follows Bonjour announcements and removals. Sleep, identity rotation, interference, and changing broadcast behavior can produce changes without physical movement. Matching names or vendors never justify combining separate identities.

What happens when a source stops?

Coverage describes source loss rather than calling every device gone. A late or recovering source gets a 30-second source-local observation window; its initial identities join that source’s comparison instead of creating a burst of apparent arrivals. A network reconnection can change which services are visible; use a new baseline when the surroundings or network have changed.

Bounded events, not a lifetime counter

Each comparison retains up to 40 recent raw change events. The display combines the same identity and change type, with retained occurrence count and first/latest times; full JSON preserves retained raw events. Counts may change as older events leave this bounded history. Radio intensity is not a measure of risk.

Capabilities and blind spots

A completed check tells you what Spectra observed—not everything that might be present.

Important to know

Powered-off, silent, wired, shielded, cellular-only, or locally recording devices may not appear. No result guarantees that a space is clear.

What to try

  1. Review Coverage to distinguish a source you did not select from one that was unavailable or only partially observed.
  2. Compare repeatable observations and inspect the surroundings. Treat device names, reflections, and magnetic spikes as clues to investigate.

Wireless visibility is limited

Bluetooth sees devices currently advertising in a form available to the app. Local Network sees supported services on your joined network—not all nearby Wi-Fi radios.

Network discovery is not covert

Local Network browsing can send queries visible to other network participants. Guest isolation, blocked multicast, or a separate network can hide devices. Do not join an unfamiliar network merely to improve a scan.

Magnetic changes are not identities

Metal, magnets, wiring, and ordinary electronics can cause excursions. A quiet magnetic trace cannot rule out a microphone, camera, or other device.

Technical detail

Foreground operation

Keep Spectra visible during measurement. A foreground interruption ends an active scan with a coverage limitation and Check ended early status; returning does not resume collection. Review retained results and Coverage before starting another check. Monitor is not an unrestricted background detector.

Busy-environment limits

Live observation buffers and the retained result list have separate limits. Repeated readings may be combined before processing; other observations may be omitted at a buffer or retention boundary, or during shutdown. Coverage distinguishes recorded reasons and retired result records; older captures may record a total without its source or reason. These counts do not measure missing devices, free disk space, or whether collection stopped. All Signals means every retained record, not every transmission in the environment.

Save, recheck, and manage sessions

Keep the checks you need, add context, and stay in control of local history.

Important to know

Deleting or resetting Spectra cannot delete exports, email attachments, or files already saved elsewhere. iOS permissions remain and are managed separately in iPhone Settings.

What to try

  1. Open Sessions, tap a date heading if needed, and choose a check by its name and time. A blue dot marks an available recheck, not an unread session.
  2. If Recheck session is available, tap it and wait for a separately saved result. Review the original Captured date, the Rechecked date, and any identification changes.
  3. Review results and Coverage, rename or add notes, then export anything you need to keep outside bounded history.
  4. Use individual, selected, or all-session deletion for the scope you intend. Read the confirmation before erasing.

Confirm a check was saved

Automatic saving of completed iPhone checks is on by default. Change Save completed sessions in Spectra Settings. Turning it off affects future iPhone checks, not existing history, deliberate imports, rechecks, or Watch sweeps. The completed check tells you whether it was saved. If saving fails, keep the results open to review, use Export JSON, or Retry Saving when offered. For a history limit, Manage Sessions lets you remove copies you no longer need before retrying. Managing history does not save or discard the open result. A forced close, crash, or shutdown before saving can lose it; there is no continuous autosaved draft. Confirm important checks were saved before closing.

Find a check by day

Sessions groups checks by their original capture day, newest first, with the newest capture times first inside each day. The most recent day starts open; tap any date heading to show or hide its sessions. Each heading counts all saved versions for that day, including rechecks. You can keep several days open, and your choices stay in place while moving between tabs or opening and returning from a session. These choices are not saved across app launches. A new most-recent day starts open; deleting all history or changing time zone can reset the day groups. Each row keeps its check-type or Watch-origin icon even after you rename it.

Read a session at a glance

Each row shows its name, scan type, original capture time and duration, collector, recorded measurement scope, and a results summary. The small icon beside the scan type is a visual cue; the text identifies the check. Partial coverage or Some measurements unavailable means the listed measurements did not all complete. Older sessions may show Source not recorded, Measurements not recorded, or Source and measurements not recorded. Spectra does not guess. If the list says Collection details in saved results, open the session to review its original metadata. Watch rows retain Experimental and any early or interrupted completion status.

Recognize a sweep collected on Watch

A Watch-with-radio-waves icon and Apple Watch · Bluetooth-only sweep label identify a session collected by the Experimental Watch companion. This is a general Bluetooth sweep from your Watch—not the Watches & Wearables specialist check on iPhone. The origin label stays visible after renaming and on rechecks. Experimental and any partial-capture status remain visible too. Original icons are blue; rechecks are violet and also say Recheck. Open Coverage to see collection limits before drawing conclusions.

Review the original capture

Open a session to see its original capture time, duration, collector, and recorded measurements. General radio results also show retained signal identities and how many have type undetermined. These totals do not change when you filter results; signal identities are not a count of physical devices. Coverage warnings remain visible. Scan breakdown contains app and recognition revisions, more detailed counts, and Session ID. Rechecks also show Original capture ID. Copy ID copies only the chosen reference on this iPhone for ten minutes; nothing is sent automatically. These IDs help reference a saved session, not authenticate its evidence. Choose Add notes to record your own context; saved notes are separate from sensor evidence.

Give a session a useful name

Open a session and choose Session options → Rename, or use its list-row menu. Names can be up to 80 characters. Clearing a custom name restores the check-type name. Notes hold up to 1,000 characters of your own context; they do not become detector evidence. A rename does not change the original scan time.

Keep rechecks with their capture

Tap the recheck count beneath an original session to show its saved interpretations, newest recheck first. Original icons are blue; recheck icons are violet and include a Recheck number. Renaming does not separate related copies. Each version opens, exports, and deletes independently. If the original was removed, the latest surviving recheck stays visible and says Original removed; any earlier rechecks remain expandable. Rechecks retain the original capture time and duration beside the scan type and show a separate evaluation date and time.

What does the blue dot mean?

A blue dot beside a session means newer recognition is available. Open it and choose Recheck session. A dot beside a collapsed recheck count means an eligible copy is inside; expand it to find that session. Spectra reinterprets compatible saved radio evidence using the engine and catalog bundled with your app update. It does not start Bluetooth or network discovery, collect new measurements, or upload your capture. Opening a session alone does not clear the dot.

Can I recheck after leaving the location?

Yes. A recheck uses saved evidence, so you do not need to return to the location, join its network, or enable Apple Intelligence. Newer recognition may explain a previously unclear signal, but better matches are not guaranteed. Rechecking cannot recover measurements that were never retained or tell you what is at that location now. Run a new check there for a new observation.

Your original stays unchanged

A successful recheck saves a separate session named from the original naming base plus Recheck 1, Recheck 2, and so on. Rechecking a recheck does not stack suffixes. Each saved copy can be renamed independently, but its original naming base remains in recheck lineage and technical exports. Rename is not an erase action. The new result separates newly identified records, changed identifications, and previous matches no longer supported. These are interpretation changes—not newly discovered devices. No identification changes is also a valid outcome. Captured shows the original measurement date; Rechecked shows when the new interpretation was created.

When is another recheck useful?

Once a current recheck is saved, the blue dot clears for that capture family; earlier copies link to the latest interpretation. A later app update can make the newest usable copy eligible again. Deleting the current interpretation can restore eligibility on a surviving older copy. A missing revision or insufficient retained evidence can prevent rechecking; Scan breakdown explains why. Some older network records lack the original discovery fields. Unsupported records and saved magnetic readings are preserved, not reconstructed or treated as fresh measurements.

Rechecks need room to save

A recheck keeps its own retained evidence, so it uses additional local storage and counts toward the session limit. It never removes an existing session to make room. Requesting a recheck explicitly saves a new copy even if automatic saving of completed scans is off. Deleting the original does not delete its independent rechecks. If saving fails, follow the recovery advice shown, then retry. Cancel or leave the screen to stop pending work; a copy already saved at the moment you cancel remains in Sessions. Erase All Sessions and Reset Spectra include rechecks.

Import a session or collection

Choose Import Sessions in Sessions and select a file in Files, or open a compatible file with Spectra. Choose one supported Technical archive JSON or a Spectra ZIP collection made with Export All Sessions; no unzipping is needed. Other ZIP files, Analysis JSON, and Reduced identifying information cannot restore sessions. Review the original capture time, recorded collector, compatibility, and integrity status for a single archive. For a collection, review how many sessions will be added and how many are already saved, then confirm the import. Cancel leaves history unchanged. Files providers may need a connection to supply your chosen file; validation and saving run on device. Imported remains separate from the original collector and capture date. Opening a file while a check or another task is in progress waits for a safe point to review it; it does not discard the open results.

A collection imports together

Import adds all eligible sessions together or adds none. Already-saved copies are left unchanged and use no additional slots. If a session has the same ID but different saved contents, Spectra reports a conflict instead of overwriting it. A damaged or unsupported archive, a conflict, or insufficient history capacity blocks the collection; Spectra never silently imports only part of it or deletes another session to make room. Original capture dates, collector details, and recheck relationships stay with the reports. Import restores recorded results, not fresh measurements, and does not rerun recognition. Importing an export without location does not remove a location from an already-saved copy.

Keep an external copy of saved sessions

Open Storage from Sessions, or Settings → Privacy & storage → Session storage, then choose Export All Sessions. Spectra prepares one ZIP collection containing a Technical archive for every session saved when preparation starts. Review the sharing options, then choose Save to Files or another destination in Apple’s share sheet. Location tags are excluded by default; enable Include scan locations only if you want to share the saved coordinates. This is not a full app backup: it excludes settings, generated AI explanations, private Bluetooth follow-up references, unsaved results, and Watch captures not yet added to Sessions. Exporting does not delete anything or free history space. Check that the external file was saved before deliberately deleting local copies.

A checksum is not authentication

New technical archives include a checksum. A matching checksum checks the archived bytes, not the author, collector, device identity, or truth of the measurements. Supported older archives can say Checksum unavailable; this is different from a failed integrity check. A failed checksum, malformed archive, or unsupported format cannot be imported. Importing does not start analysis, scanning, a recheck, or a map, and does not change the source file. Private Bluetooth follow-up references are not included in archives, so a later proximity check may require confirming a possible match.

Review or remove a saved location

If you enabled Tag location and a usable fix arrived, Scan start location shows the iPhone’s recorded coordinates and estimated accuracy. On your first tagged result, choose Enable map previews to show saved areas automatically through Apple Maps, or Not now to keep coordinates only. Your choice is remembered. Change Automatic map previews in the Session maps section of Spectra Settings whenever you like. The marker describes the collecting iPhone; the shaded area represents its estimated accuracy, not where detected devices are. The preview does not request your current location or confirm a venue or room. Open in Maps is a separate choice for exploring the saved position in Maps. Maps opens only when you choose; previews load automatically only after you enable them. Rechecks inherit the selected copy’s original capture location, never your current position. A session without a tag cannot be assigned today’s location as though it were captured there. Watch sweeps remain untagged.

When a map cannot load

Saved coordinates and findings remain available if the optional preview fails or a connection is unavailable. Choose Retry map preview when offered, or Open in Maps to use Maps. Some regions near the poles cannot be represented by this preview; retry is not offered for an unsupported location. Spectra clears its temporary preview when you leave the results or switch away from the app; when you return, it can load again if Automatic map previews remains enabled. A failed preview does not continuously retry. Map requests are handled under Apple’s data practices. Clearing Spectra’s preview does not erase information or caches held by Apple Maps.

A tag belongs to each saved copy

Choose Session options → Remove location and confirm to remove the tag from this copy without deleting its findings. Other sessions, rechecks, and exported files stay unchanged; remove their tags separately if needed. Protected recovery history can still contain an earlier copy. Use Erase All Sessions or Reset Spectra to clear local session and recovery history. Neither action recalls files already shared.

Compare saved sizes

Each saved-session row includes Estimated size to help you compare larger saved sessions before deleting. It includes recorded capture data and that session’s saved list information, such as its name and notes. KiB and MiB use binary units. Shared history information, protected recovery copies, received Watch captures and exports are separate, so row estimates do not add up to the history total and deletion may free a different amount. A recheck or imported copy has its own size. An unavailable estimate is not shown; it does not mean the session is empty.

Make room without losing another session

Sessions shows the saved count and primary history-file usage. Tap the upper Storage section for details; Import Sessions below is a separate action. You can also choose Session storage under Privacy & storage in Spectra Settings. History keeps up to 500 sessions within 256 MiB, with originals, rechecks, and imported copies counted separately. These limits are independent: free bytes do not provide another session slot, and an available slot does not guarantee a report will fit. Saving or importing never removes another session to make room. Export important copies, then delete those you no longer need before retrying. Exporting alone does not free history space. On this iPhone separately measures local capture files, including protected recovery, pending history files, and received Watch captures; it is not total app storage or free device space. Local history is marked for exclusion from backups; iOS controls backup and restore behavior. Export destinations have their own storage and backup policies.

Swipe to rename or delete

Swipe right on a session to reveal Rename, or swipe fully to open the name editor. Nothing changes until you save. Swipe left to reveal Delete, or swipe fully to delete that session. Undo briefly restores the most recently deleted session, including its name, notes, location tag, and retained evidence. It is available for 10 seconds, or 30 seconds when VoiceOver is running at deletion. Another deletion replaces the previous Undo opportunity. Leaving Sessions, switching away from Spectra, or starting confirmed bulk erasure or reset ends Undo. Spectra removes the saved copy first and keeps only one temporary in-memory recovery copy; there is no Recently Deleted folder.

Choose the right erase action

Use Select to delete several sessions with confirmation. Hiding a day or collapsing rechecks does not deselect hidden sessions: the date heading and recheck disclosure keep their selected counts, and Delete Selected shows the total. Expand the relevant group to change its selection. Deleting an original does not select or delete its rechecks. Erase All Sessions includes closed groups, recovery copies, and received Watch inbox captures; it is disabled when there is nothing to erase. Watch copies remain; this action does not request a Watch reset. Use Settings → Experimental Watch → Erase Inbox Copies to clear that inbox separately without deleting imported sessions. Reset Spectra additionally clears received Watch diagnostics and preferences, returns to Welcome, and requests a reset of the known paired Watch app. Check its separate pending or confirmed status. Delete Selected, Erase All Sessions, and Reset Spectra clear pending Undo and cannot be undone.

Technical detail

Storage is temporarily unavailable

Unlock the iPhone and return to Spectra or retry saving. A temporary read failure pauses history changes and is retried; it does not trigger damaged-file recovery or authorize overwriting existing sessions. If the problem persists, keep any unsaved result open and export it if possible before contacting support.

History cannot be read

Spectra pauses history changes to avoid overwriting unreadable data. Preserve Copy and Start Fresh keeps a protected recovery copy before creating new history. This is not a promise that damaged data can be recovered. Erase Stored Data is the separate destructive choice. Contact support if a report matters before erasing.

Old results are historical

Sessions groups each capture and its rechecks by the original capture time in your current local calendar. Rechecking does not move that capture to a newer day. Recheck rows show their separate evaluation date and time; details preserve the original measurement date and duration. Captured with identifies the original app build; Recognition used or Rechecked with identifies the interpretation revisions. Older reports may show Not recorded. Opening them does not silently reinterpret their findings, fill in today’s version, or invent missing graph data.

Analyze findings and export JSON

Explain one finding on device, share or copy it for another AI app, or export JSON. Choose what to share; nothing uploads automatically.

Important to know

A JSON report is not a packet capture, continuous recording, authenticated device inventory, or tamper-evident forensic record. An AI can make mistakes; review its conclusions against the actual evidence.

What to try

  1. To explain one finding, use Analyze on Device when available or Use Another App under Analyze a finding. The latter lets you review, then share or copy the prepared text. Both options are independent of JSON export.
  2. To export a saved session, choose Export JSON from the top Session options menu. After a completed check, use the Export results section.
  3. Select one format: Analysis JSON, Technical archive, or Reduced identifying information. Read what is included. Include scan location, when offered, is a separate choice and starts off.
  4. Tap Export JSON and wait for preparation to finish. In the share sheet, choose Save to Files or a recipient you trust.

Explore first, then choose a tool

Completed checks and saved sessions put findings and measurements first. Below them, Bluetooth proximity lets you investigate an eligible recorded Bluetooth identity while nearby. Analyze a finding offers Analyze on Device and Use Another App. The latter prepares one finding for review before you choose Share or Copy. Export results saves or shares the scan. These are independent options, not required steps. A device’s details can open proximity or analysis with that finding already selected.

Scan type sets the scope—not screen filters

Scan type sets the export scope and available analysis choices—not the visible results filters. Smart Glasses and Watches & Wearables include supported catalog matches and explicitly Possible name-based device-type indications, with their associated evidence. The wearable check includes watches, fitness bands and trackers, smart rings, and other supported wearables; glasses remain separate. Unrelated activity is excluded. With no matches, exported results are empty; scan type, duration, and coverage remain available. Quick Check, Area Sweep, and Monitor exports include all retained data for their scan type. On-device analysis explains only the single finding you select. Acquisition counts are observations, not device counts.

Analysis JSON

The default export is a compact, model-friendly evidence document with scan-local citations, identity hypotheses, interpreted evidence, radio summaries, magnetic detector events, and Monitor changes. Coverage and user notes are separate context. It omits raw packet bytes, display-only graphs, and all generated AI explanations. This file is for your own external analysis workflow; the on-device feature uses a smaller, separate summary of just one finding.

Technical archive

Includes all retained results within the scan type, confidence, advertised names and payloads, coverage, catalog metadata, session name and notes, retained Monitor events and comparisons, and a saved magnetic graph when applicable. It ignores the visible results filter. Use this for detailed technical review or a supported Import Sessions restore. Export All Sessions packages these Technical archives in one Spectra ZIP collection. New archives include a checksum; it does not authenticate the author or observations. The file may include personal information. Generated AI explanations are excluded, including any analysis retained by an earlier app version.

Reduced identifying information

Keeps device categories, typed confidence, signal measurements, relative timing, source-coverage states, and the collector platform when recorded. By default it omits location, collector hardware and OS version, original identifiers, absolute timestamps, names, notes, manufacturers, model labels, raw payloads, and free-text evidence/limitations. App and recognition versions remain, including the original capture build for rechecks. Choosing Include scan location adds the saved coordinates, accuracy, and absolute location timestamp to this otherwise reduced file. Measurement patterns can still reveal context, so reduced does not mean anonymous. The missing context can limit an analyst’s conclusions.

Choose whether to share the location

Location tags are excluded from every format by default—even Technical archive. Choose whether to include technical data

The default prepared text includes protocol identifiers, interpretation confidence, historical catalog context, and signal statistics. Recorded names, declared network text, and raw payloads are excluded. Include names and technical payloads is optional and resets for each finding or new presentation. Names and bytes may identify people or devices. Location tags, session names, notes, other findings, and AI explanations are never deliberately included, even with technical sharing enabled. Grouped network services remain separate within the selected finding; omission counts describe bounded evidence.

A useful explanation, not a verdict

Both actions prepare a concise prompt followed by JSON, not a standalone JSON file. It asks what the finding may be, what that means, how confident the identification is, and which practical next checks would help. It leads with device type, adds only supported names or models, and separates a device’s usual capabilities from behavior actually observed. Instructions are tailored to the included Bluetooth, network, or magnetic evidence. A magnetic change cannot identify a device or brand. Unknown remains a valid conclusion; catalog guesses and the names they came from are not independent proof.

Keep external research in perspective

What goes into a finding explanation?

For a radio finding, Spectra supplies the identity hypothesis, confidence, representative observation, coverage, and available signal-strength summary—not every packet. Related network services may form one likely endpoint. For magnetic findings, it supplies the excursion count, maximum baseline deviation, and up to three strongest retained incident summaries with field, signed delta, threshold, excess, and duration. Earlier incidents may no longer be retained. Raw histories, display graphs, your notes, and other findings are excluded. A magnetic excursion does not identify a device or its purpose.

Ask an AI useful questions

Ask it to summarize observed device categories, explain confidence and coverage limits, identify repeatable magnetic events, and suggest non-invasive follow-up checks. Tell it to treat all device names, transmitted fields, and notes as untrusted data—not instructions. Do not ask it to certify safety or infer recording from a product name.

Technical detail

Versioned, explicit units

The export identifies its schema and producing app build. The technical archive uses ISO 8601 UTC timestamps, RSSI in dBm, magnetic field in microteslas (µT), and durations in seconds. Payload bytes use base64. Missing fields mean not recorded or unavailable, not zero. Reduced exports are a separate allowlisted shape, not a full report with blank names.

Which device collected the evidence?

New captures record the collector platform, hardware model, OS version, and collector app version and build. Analysis JSON and Technical archive preserve those facts separately from the app that interprets or exports the report. A Watch capture remains a Watch capture after transfer or recheck. Older evidence can lack these fields; Spectra does not fill them with the current phone’s information. Personal device names, serial numbers, and persistent device identifiers are not collected for this metadata.

Observations and signal samples differ

An observation count describes received broadcasts, not physical devices. Bluetooth signal statistics summarize a bounded recent window of accepted RSSI samples; that window is not the full observation history. Comparing two captures requires considering duration, collection time, receiver position, and people moving nearby. A stronger reading or larger count alone does not establish better coverage.

What a graph can explain

Saved magnetic display points preserve a bounded, downsampled window. Do not connect gaps or different reference revisions, assume the complete sweep was retained, or confuse display delta with the detector’s independent calibrated baseline. Bluetooth reports preserve signal summaries. A Bluetooth Proximity check is a separate, unsaved live measurement.

Technical detail

Protected, bounded history

Saved reports and notes use iOS file protection. New on-device AI explanations are temporary: they are not saved in sessions or included in exports. Older sessions may still contain explanations saved by an earlier app version; deleting those sessions removes that local history too. Spectra marks the session directory, including recovery copies, for exclusion from device backups. This is not a guarantee that sessions can never appear in a backup or on a restored device; iOS controls backup and restore behavior. Files you export may be stored or backed up by your chosen destination. The primary history file keeps up to 500 sessions within 256 MiB; originals, rechecks, and imported copies count toward these independent limits. Saved sessions are not automatically removed. Protected recovery copies also remain until deliberate erasure; Erase All Sessions and Reset Spectra include them. Newer local history formats may not open in older app builds. Return to a compatible build rather than erasing history you still need to resolve a version mismatch. Safe file replacement, protected recovery copies, and separate Watch inbox data can require additional disk space. The displayed history usage is not total app storage or an export file’s size. These are not tamper-evident forensic records or an unlimited archive.

Session identifiers and saved signal checks

It contains no raw Bluetooth framework identifier and is never included in JSON, external finding shares or copies, or Watch transfers. Its private index is marked for exclusion from device backups, subject to iOS backup behavior. Rechecks of that capture share its reference. Removing the last non-imported original or recheck copy retires its active local reference; imported copies cannot keep it. Undo can briefly restore it with the session. Troubleshoot unexpected results

First check coverage and measurement state. A missing observation is not evidence that nothing is there.

Important to know

For a repeatable problem, contact support@arcsignal.zendesk.com or open Support site in Spectra Settings. Start with support information, the steps taken, and what you expected. Do not send private scan results unless you deliberately choose to share them.

What to try

  1. Open Coverage and read the reason for each partial or unavailable measurement.
  2. Check iPhone Settings if access was denied, return to Spectra, and repeat the smallest affected check.
  3. In Spectra Settings, open Support information and choose Copy support information. Return to Settings for Support site or Email support. The copy includes version, build, iOS, hardware model, detection-engine revision and catalog revision—not scan data.

My known device does not appear

The device may be silent, sleeping, already connected without recognizable advertisements, on another network, or outside current recognition coverage. Some watches expose more information only in discovery or workout modes. Use general Quick Check or Area Sweep to include unclassified activity; specialist checks show only supported matches. Longer listening may help an intermittent signal but cannot reveal fields the device or iOS does not supply. Do not connect to unknown equipment to force a result.

Why a nearby watch or ring can remain unknown

An Apple, Garmin, Samsung, or other company code alone does not identify a wearable. Generic heart-rate services can also come from chest straps. Distinctive names can support a possible product match; selected historical protocol patterns remain explicitly uncertain and may not appear on current firmware. Watches & Wearables includes supported watches, fitness bands, smart rings, and other wearables. A silent device or a generic service can remain unclassified; use a general check to inspect that activity. A wearable match cannot tell whether a microphone is active.

What do the live category counts mean?

iPhone scans use the same recognition rules for live counts and completed results. Counts refresh as observations are processed. Finishing organizes the retained evidence; it does not run a stronger scan. Watches & wearables counts supported wearable signal identities, not every nearby wearable. Other & unclassified may include wearables without a supported match. One physical device can broadcast several identities. Opening a saved session keeps its recorded interpretation. When available, Recheck session creates a separate copy using newer recognition without changing the original.

The name or type looks wrong

A broadcast name is self-reported and can be renamed or copied. Company identifiers sometimes identify a chip or protocol namespace, not the retail manufacturer. Possible and Probable are evidence labels, not numeric probabilities. Conflicting product claims stay ambiguous; details explain the supporting evidence. Catalog updates ship with app updates; Spectra does not download identification data during a scan.

The magnetic reading barely changes near a device

Some devices produce little detectable magnetic change. Sensor position, distance, shielding, and magnetic accessories can also affect the response. No change does not rule out a device. The sensor is a small point inside the phone, not its entire back. See Reading the magnetic meter for positioning and a safe comparison exercise. On iPhone 17 Pro Max, use the lower back near the USB-C end, not the camera area. Check that live readings are arriving before interpreting a flat graph.

The graph moves but no spike is saved

Actual field and display delta can work before the calibrated event detector is ready. A 0/25-style sample count describes accepted detector-baseline readings, not permission progress. Follow the current readiness instruction, pause away from obvious magnets, and check Measurement Details. Strong absolute field alone is not a qualifying relative excursion. Try removing a magnetic case or mount if practical.

Wi-Fi works but no devices appear

Internet access does not prove local discovery works. Guest isolation, blocked multicast, unadvertised service types, separate subnets, or a denied Local Network permission can prevent results. Check Coverage and the joined network. Spectra is not a scanner for all Wi-Fi radios and does not probe arbitrary ports.

A check stopped or looks incomplete

Keep Spectra visible. Locking, switching apps, permission changes, source loss, and ending early can limit a check. Check ended early marks a foreground interruption; returning does not resume the check. Check interrupted offers Review Coverage to inspect the problem and Review Results to finish reviewing available measurements, even if there are no signal findings. Confirm the separate saved status before closing. Restore access if needed, then start a fresh check from Scan. An incomplete check is not a no-match result. On a busy scan, wait until collection finishes to explore stable detailed results.

Does an observation notice mean storage is full?

Some observations were not retained does not by itself mean the result list or device storage is full. Read recorded reasons in Coverage. Repeated readings can be combined routinely; inventory and endpoint counts have different units. Result retention limit reached means some observations could not add a new result record at that limit. Result records retired means earlier records were removed from the bounded retained list. Retained results remain available; these notices alone do not require restarting or prove collection stopped. Older captures may not record a breakdown.

A file cannot be imported

Choose a supported Technical archive JSON or a Spectra ZIP collection, not an arbitrary ZIP, Analysis JSON, or Reduced identifying information. A collection with a conflict, invalid member, or insufficient capacity is not partially imported. Already-saved copies are left unchanged. A failed checksum or malformed file is different from an older archive with Checksum unavailable. Use the original exporter to create another technical archive when possible; a newer unsupported schema may require a Spectra update. If history is full, manage saved sessions before retrying; Spectra does not remove existing history for an import. If file or protected-storage access is unavailable, unlock the iPhone and retry the appropriate access step. Canceling file selection is not an import failure.
