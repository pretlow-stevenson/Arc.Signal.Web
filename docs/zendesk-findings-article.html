Hardware icons, separate confidence

Device icons describe a possible hardware category, not activity. A reviewed MacBook match can use a laptop; an uncertain subtype uses its supported parent category. Unknown type, observed source

An otherwise unclassified radio finding uses a neutral source icon: radio waves for Bluetooth, a network for local-network observations, or a dotted circle for mixed or unavailable radio-source information. The amber Similarity only caution still means the identities may be separate devices; blue does not make their relationship or identity certain.

Read the confidence label

  • Possible and Probable describe supporting evidence, not numeric probabilities.
  • System name may be cached by the collecting device’s Bluetooth framework rather than transmitted in that observation.
  • Conflicting identity claims means Spectra has not selected a product.
  • Company or service codes can identify a registered namespace without identifying the finished device.

Names and broadcasts can be changed or spoofed. Technical details preserve supporting fields and recognition sources.

Registered services are not device names

Google-registered Bluetooth service means Google registered that service code—not that Google made the nearby device. Device unknown means Spectra has not identified the device. The label is not an account-registration message or evidence that the device is communicating with Google.

Useful clues inside a broadcast

Complete, reviewed advertisement formats can add useful context without identifying an exact product.

  • Nearby Presence broadcast identifies a protocol format, not a phone, watch, or wearable.
  • Possible phone · Nearby sharing broadcast describes a declared category in a compatible sharing format. It does not establish the maker, model, operating system, owner, or current activity.
  • Possible HomeKit camera describes a public accessory-category claim. HomeKit’s Apple-registered company code does not mean Apple manufactured the accessory, and the claim does not prove recording.

These clues are unauthenticated and can be copied. Unknown, incomplete, and unsupported formats remain uninterpreted. Encrypted contents and account information stay opaque; rotating identifiers are not resolved into an owner or stable device identity. Technical details preserve service codes, parser outcomes, sources, and recognition revisions for further review.

Reviewed product and family names help identify both Bluetooth and local-network observations. A recognizable Apple TV name, for example, can suggest the same family on either source. A network role without an exact model

Supported printing, scanning, file-sharing, media, automation, and imaging or audio services can explain what a network endpoint advertises even when its device is unknown. A service is not necessarily physical hardware: a computer can host a print queue, and an imaging service may also belong to audio hardware. Reviewed product names and protocol-specific model fields can support a Possible family or model. Conflicting or unsupported fields remain qualified; Spectra does not connect to endpoints or scan arbitrary ports to identify them.

Bluetooth strength is not distance

Less-negative dBm values mean a stronger received signal: −40 dBm is stronger than −80 dBm. Walls, people, antenna orientation, and transmit power affect readings. A stronger signal is not necessarily the closest device.
