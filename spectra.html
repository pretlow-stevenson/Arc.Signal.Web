For unfamiliar places

A little context.
A more informed next step.

Privacy matters wherever you go. Start with what you can observe, then decide whether a closer look is worthwhile—not whether an app has declared a place safe.

Hotels & vacation rentals

Get oriented at a hotel or Airbnb with a Quick Check. Use an untimed Area Sweep to explore accessible areas you are allowed to inspect. A repeatable magnetic change is a reason to look more closely, not proof of a hidden camera.

Shared & personal spaces

In student housing, gyms, and changing rooms, privacy boundaries matter. Observe available broadcasts without pairing, follow facility rules, and review findings away from others. Do not photograph people, dismantle fixtures, or treat an unfamiliar signal as wrongdoing.

Travel & public places

As a rideshare passenger or while waiting in an airport, station, or café, take a quick radio snapshot or check for recognizable glasses and wearables. Crowded spaces contain many ordinary devices. A match cannot identify their owner or tell whether they are recording.

Signals can come from outside the room; silent devices may not appear at all. If you have a credible concern, leave sensitive activity for a more appropriate location and contact a trusted safety or security resource. Do not confront someone based on a scan.

01 / Quick Check

Get your bearings.

Take a roughly 30-second look at nearby Bluetooth broadcasts, with optional local-network observations. No magnetic measurement or calibration is needed.

02 / Area Sweep

Explore at your pace.

Move through the area at your own pace, compare Bluetooth signals, and optionally follow the live magnetic meter. Finish when you’re ready, without a countdown.

03 / Monitor This Area

Notice what changes.

Keep the phone stationary. Each selected, available radio observes for 30 seconds to establish a baseline; you can extend this once to 60 seconds. Glance at change counts and the latest activity, then Finish for details. Foreground monitoring only—not an unattended alarm.

How it works

Look beyond a
single signal.

01

Bluetooth

Recognize available broadcasts. After a check, choose a found Bluetooth identity from grouped results and open Bluetooth Proximity for its live signal-strength reading. Less-negative dBm means stronger reception: −45 dBm is stronger than −75 dBm.

Signal strength—not an exact distance or direction.

02

Local network

Find advertised services on the Wi-Fi network you’ve joined, from speakers and streaming receivers to smart-home products.

Network presence—not a scan of every nearby Wi-Fi radio.

03

Magnetic field

Available in Area Sweep. See actual field readings, then compare changes from a stable reference. A higher actual µT value means a stronger field; a larger positive or negative delta means a bigger change from the reference, not a distance. Quality-gated spike detection uses its own calibrated baseline. Revisit the saved, downsampled graph after the check.

A field change—not proof of a particular device. Ordinary magnets and metal can cause changes too.

Beyond the scan

Awareness is one layer.
Good habits add more.

Spectra observes nearby electronic clues. Protecting your accounts, connections, and conversations is a separate part of staying informed.

Check the connection.

Confirm the intended Wi-Fi name with the venue through a trusted channel. A familiar name is not authentication. Prefer cellular data if you do not trust the network; do not join unfamiliar Wi-Fi just to improve a scan. HTTPS protects traffic to a site, not the trustworthiness of the site itself.

Use a VPN thoughtfully.

A trusted VPN can protect traffic routed through its encrypted tunnel on shared Wi-Fi; trust shifts to the VPN provider. HTTPS already protects much web traffic. A VPN does not stop nearby recording, authenticate a hotspot, guarantee anonymity, or make malicious sites safe.

Protect the basics.

Keep iOS and apps updated, use unique passwords and multifactor authentication, and heed certificate warnings. Protect sensitive screens and conversations. Follow your organization’s security policy; do not disable a required VPN to improve local-network discovery. Bluetooth and magnetic checks do not need that network.

Good questions

Know what a
finding means.

Can Spectra tell me a room is safe?

No. Spectra reveals available signals, not a safety verdict. Silent, powered-off, wired, shielded, cellular-only, or locally recording devices may not appear. Use findings as a reason to look more closely.

Does a product name confirm the device?

No. Names and metadata can be changed or spoofed. Matches are possible or probable identities, with the underlying observations available for review.

Do I need to connect to a device to inspect it?

No pairing or direct connection is needed. Local-network discovery requires joining the network and granting permission. Results depend on your chosen sources and device capabilities.

Why might a saved session have no recheck option?

Recheck appears only when newer compatible recognition is available and the session retains enough usable radio evidence. A fresh capture already using the installed recognition normally needs no recheck. Some older or incomplete captures cannot be reassessed. Catalog updates arrive with app releases—not separate downloads. Rechecking needs no Apple Intelligence and cannot recover missing measurements or tell you what is at that location now.
